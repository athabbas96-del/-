# Imagery and photography

Every style has a photography direction (summary, rules, three sample shots,
what to avoid). This guide covers what applies to all of them.

## 1. Writing a shot brief

A brief turns a style's direction into a shoot. Each shot needs:

| Field | Example (Organic Earth, fictional brand Fernhollow) |
| --- | --- |
| Shot | Hands pouring oil over sage leaves |
| Purpose and placement | Website hero, right column; also cropped 4:5 for feed |
| Framing | Close-up, 45° from above |
| Light | Soft window light from the left, gentle shadow |
| Palette / grade | Sage, moss, oat; slightly warm |
| Negative space | Top-left third empty for headline |
| Props and set | Linen, river stone, amber bottle |
| Talent | Hands only; natural nails, no jewellery |
| Deliverables | 3 crops: 4:5, 1:1, 16:9; RAW + graded TIFF |
| Avoid | Cold light, plastic packaging, white seamless |

The photo frames in the library are art-direction placeholders: their colours
are the target grade and their caption is the shot line of the brief.

## 2. Cropping for every format

Shoot wider than you need, with the subject away from the edges, so one image
yields every crop: 1:1 and 4:5 feed, 9:16 story, 16:9 web hero, 2:1 billboard.
Mark a "safe" area in the brief that survives all crops.

## 3. Retouching and ethics

- Retouch to clean, not to change bodies. Do not alter skin tone, body shape or
  age beyond removing temporary blemishes unless the talent agrees.
- Health, beauty and finance imagery must not imply results that aren't real.
- Show people with dignity; avoid stereotypes. Cast to the client's real audience.
- Respect cultural context: dress, gestures and settings appropriate to the market.

## 4. Rights and releases

- Every identifiable person needs a signed model release covering the planned
  uses (channels, territories, duration).
- Private property and some public landmarks need a property release.
- Stock: record the licence type, licence ID and any restrictions in the project
  folder. Editorial-only images cannot be used in advertising.
- AI-generated or AI-edited images: confirm the client accepts them, check the
  tool's commercial terms, never generate real people or imitate a living
  photographer's signature style, and disclose where the platform or market
  requires it.

## 5. Alt text

Write what the image *communicates* in context, in one sentence.
"Hands pouring rosehip oil onto sage leaves", not "image of a bottle".
Decorative images get empty alt text.

## Checklist

- [ ] Brief written per shot, with negative space for copy
- [ ] Shot wide enough for every crop
- [ ] Retouching limited to temporary blemishes
- [ ] Model, property and stock licences recorded
- [ ] Alt text written
