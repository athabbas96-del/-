# Pitch decks

## 1. Structure for a creative pitch

| # | Section | Slides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Cover: client, project, date | 1 |
| 2 | What we heard: the brief in the client's words | 1–2 |
| 3 | Insight: what we found that they may not know | 2–3 |
| 4 | Strategy: the one-sentence direction | 1 |
| 5 | Creative directions: two or three routes, each with a name, idea, moodboard and key visual | 3–5 per route |
| 6 | How it works across channels: social, ads, web, presentations | 3–4 |
| 7 | Plan: timeline, team, budget | 2–3 |
| 8 | Next steps | 1 |

**Using the library:** each style can be presented as a creative route. Use its
cover, palette, typography and rendered examples as the route's moodboard,
then replace the sample brand with the client's name and logo before the
meeting. Never present sample brands as client work.

## 2. Writing slides

- **Action titles:** the title states the conclusion ("Waiting times fell by
  13 minutes"), not the topic ("Waiting times"). Someone reading only the
  titles should get the argument (the Pyramid Principle).
- One message per slide; supporting points, not paragraphs.
- Presenter notes hold the detail; the slide holds the point.
- Rule of thumb from Guy Kawasaki's 10/20/30: few slides, short talk, and
  type no smaller than 30pt when presenting to a room. For decks that are sent
  rather than presented, 18–24pt body is acceptable.

## 3. Design

- Use the client's or the chosen style's master layouts
  ([Slide layouts](../slide-layouts/README.md)).
- Full-bleed images to change pace between sections.
- One chart idea per slide; highlight the data point that proves the title.

## Checklist

- [ ] Titles alone tell the story
- [ ] Routes named, each with idea, moodboard and key visual
- [ ] Sample brand content replaced with the client's
- [ ] Type size fits presented vs sent use
