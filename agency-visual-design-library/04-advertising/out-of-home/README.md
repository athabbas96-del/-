# Out of home (OOH)

## 1. Formats

Sizes vary by country and media owner. Always design from the media owner's
spec sheet. Common UK sheet sizes, for reference:

| Format | Size | Ratio | Typical site |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6-sheet | 1200 × 1800 mm | 2:3 | Bus shelters, malls, street furniture |
| 48-sheet | 6096 × 3048 mm | 2:1 | Roadside; rendered on every style page |
| 96-sheet | 12192 × 3048 mm | 4:1 | Major roads |
| Digital screens | Per owner, e.g. 1080 × 1920 or 1920 × 1080 | | Malls, airports, metro, roadside |

## 2. Designing for distance and speed

- **Seven words or fewer** for roadside; people see it for a few seconds.
- **Letter height rule of thumb:** about 25mm of letter height per 3m of
  viewing distance (the sign industry's "1 inch per 10 feet"). Treat it as a
  minimum, and check the media owner's viewing-distance figures for the site.
- One image, one line, one logo. No body copy, no small print except legal lines.
- High contrast: billboards are seen in glare, rain and at night.
- Put the brand early in the reading order; a billboard without a visible brand
  advertises the category.

## 3. Placement as part of the idea

The library's campaigns use placement deliberately: Kinetic Sport places
"Run the City" on running routes; Quiet Luxury avoids cluttered roadside sites
in favour of airports and hotel lobbies; Modern Heritage books lantern-lit
evening sites during Ramadan.

## 4. Production

- Artwork at scale (often 1:10) at the resolution the printer asks for.
- Bleed and visible area per the spec; frames can hide 30–50mm of the edges.
- Digital screens: check static vs motion rules, dwell time and brightness;
  some cities restrict motion on roadside screens.

## Checklist

- [ ] Media owner spec used for size, bleed and visible area
- [ ] Seven words or fewer; letter size tested for distance
- [ ] Brand visible early; strong contrast
- [ ] Digital rules (motion, dwell) checked
